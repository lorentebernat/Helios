# 14 — Naval Combat & Defense

> While **Anno 1800** is primarily an economic and city-building game, naval supremacy remains essential for protecting trade routes, defending your islands, and projecting power across the map. A strong navy safeguards your economy and provides strategic flexibility throughout the campaign.

This chapter summarizes the naval combat and defensive systems described in the source guide.

---

# Overview

Naval power serves five major purposes:

- Defend trade routes
- Protect islands
- Destroy enemy fleets
- Conquer islands
- Eliminate pirate threats

Although peaceful play is entirely viable, every empire benefits from maintaining a capable defensive fleet.

---

# Naval Warfare

Combat occurs entirely at sea.

Ships engage using:

- Cannons
- Ship abilities
- Equipped items
- Tactical positioning

Victory depends not only on fleet size but also on ship quality and positioning.

---

# Fleet Composition

A balanced fleet normally contains:

```text
Heavy Warships

+

Medium Combat Ships

+

Support Ships
```

Different vessels excel in different situations.

Rather than relying on a single powerful ship, coordinated fleets are generally more effective.

---

# Strategic Objectives

Military fleets should focus on protecting:

- Trade routes
- Oil transport
- High-value cargo
- Harbor entrances
- Expedition routes

Losing a major cargo route can disrupt multiple production chains simultaneously.

---

# Trade Route Protection

Priority routes include:

```text
Coffee

↓

Engineers
```

```text
Rubber

↓

Advanced Industry
```

```text
Oil

↓

Power Plants
```

Interruptions to these routes can have severe economic consequences because many late-game production chains depend on imported resources. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# Harbor Defense

Every important island should eventually develop permanent defenses.

Typical defensive buildings include:

- Coastal Cannons
- Heavy Coastal Batteries
- Defensive Towers
- Harbor Defenses

These structures discourage enemy attacks and support friendly fleets.

The guide introduces harbor defensive structures as part of advanced harbor development. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Defensive Layers

A well-protected island usually combines:

```text
Harbor Defenses

↓

Defensive Fleet

↓

Repair Facilities

↓

Supply Network
```

Multiple defensive layers provide significantly greater security than relying on ships alone.

---

# Repair Facilities

Repair Cranes provide several benefits:

- repair docked ships
- reduce downtime
- improve fleet readiness

Major naval bases should always include repair infrastructure.

---

# Ship Positioning

Good positioning can greatly influence combat effectiveness.

General principles:

- avoid fighting inside enemy harbor range
- focus fire on individual targets
- protect damaged vessels
- prevent enemy access to vulnerable cargo ships

---

# Pirate Threats

Pirates present an ongoing threat to commerce.

Common responses include:

- escort fleets
- patrol routes
- diplomacy
- eliminating pirate bases

The appropriate strategy depends on your military strength and economic priorities.

---

# Convoys

High-value cargo should often travel under escort.

Typical convoy:

```text
Warship

↓

Cargo Ship

↓

Warship
```

This arrangement reduces the risk of losing valuable shipments.

---

# Naval Bases

Large empires often benefit from establishing dedicated naval bases.

Typical facilities include:

- Shipyard
- Repair Crane
- Harbor Defenses
- Storage Depots

Centralized fleet management simplifies maintenance and deployment.

---

# Harbormaster Support

Harbormaster's Offices may improve naval operations through specialist items.

Possible bonuses include:

- faster repairs
- stronger defenses
- improved loading
- reduced maintenance
- additional harbor productivity

These bonuses affect nearby harbor buildings within the office's area of influence. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# Naval Logistics During War

War increases logistical demands.

Monitor:

- replacement ships
- repair capacity
- construction materials
- ammunition supply (where applicable)
- protected trade routes

Economic stability remains the foundation of military success.

---

# Island Conquest

Capturing islands provides access to:

- fertile land
- mineral deposits
- strategic locations
- additional harbor capacity

However, conquest also creates new logistical responsibilities.

Expansion should always be supported by a stable economy.

---

# Economic Warfare

Disrupting enemy logistics can be more effective than direct confrontation.

Potential targets include:

- cargo ships
- oil routes
- regional imports
- strategic harbors

Since advanced production chains depend heavily on imported goods, disrupting logistics can severely weaken an opponent's economy.

---

# Fleet Modernization

As technology advances:

Replace:

```text
Sailing Ships

↓

Steam Ships
```

Modern fleets offer:

- higher durability
- greater speed
- improved cargo protection

The guide consistently presents steam technology as the superior late-game option. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

# Common Mistakes

⚠️ Ignoring naval defenses.

⚠️ Leaving trade routes unprotected.

⚠️ Building only cargo ships.

⚠️ Fighting inside enemy harbor defenses.

⚠️ Neglecting repair facilities.

⚠️ Delaying fleet modernization.

---

- [ ] Storage capacity

---

## Every Strategic Trade Route

- [ ] Escort (when necessary)
- [ ] Backup logistics

---

## Empire-Wide

- [ ] Modern fleet
- [ ] Protected oil routes
- [ ] Protected luxury imports
- [ ] Reserve construction materials

---

# Key Takeaways

- A strong navy protects the economy as much as it wins battles.
- Harbor defenses and repair infrastructure are essential for important islands.
- Protect high-value trade routes, especially those supplying electricity and luxury production.
- Modern steam fleets outperform early sailing vessels in nearly every strategic role.
- Naval strength should support economic expansion rather than replace it.
