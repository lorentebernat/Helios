# 02 — Economy

> This chapter explains the economic systems that drive **Anno 1800**. A successful empire is built on balancing income, expenses, production, workforce, and logistics. Every decision ultimately affects your financial stability.

---

# Economic Philosophy

Unlike many city builders, money in Anno 1800 is not generated simply by population size.

Your economy depends on:

- Population
- Needs fulfilled
- Luxury goods supplied
- Production costs
- Workforce
- Maintenance
- Trade
- Efficiency

A city with fewer but fully supplied residents will generally outperform a much larger city lacking essential goods.

---

# The Economic Loop

```text
Expand Population
        │
        ▼
Increase Needs
        │
        ▼
Build Production
        │
        ▼
Consume Goods
        │
        ▼
Collect Taxes
        │
        ▼
Increase Income
        │
        ▼
Unlock New Population
```

Every new population tier increases both your potential tax income and the complexity of your production network.

---

# Income

Income primarily comes from residents.

Residents pay taxes based on:

- Population tier
- Fulfilled basic needs
- Fulfilled luxury needs

Higher social classes contribute significantly more income than lower classes.

Approximate profitability follows this order:

```text
Farmers
    ↓
Workers
    ↓
Artisans
    ↓
Engineers
    ↓
Investors
```

The guide identifies investors as the highest-income social class in the Old World and notes that they also increase your empire's influence. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# Expenses

Every production building has maintenance costs.

Typical expenses include:

- Production buildings
- Public services
- Ships
- Military
- Harbour buildings
- Electricity infrastructure
- Railways
- Monuments

Large industrial districts can consume enormous amounts of money if they produce unnecessary goods.

---

# Supply and Demand

One of the biggest mistakes beginners make is producing more than necessary.

Instead:

```text
Demand
      │
      ▼
Required Production
      │
      ▼
Required Workforce
      │
      ▼
Required Maintenance
```

Always build according to actual consumption whenever possible.

---

# Workforce

Every production building requires workers.

Different buildings require different population tiers.

Example progression:

| Population | Typical Roles |
|------------|---------------|
| Farmers | Agriculture |
| Workers | Heavy industry |
| Artisans | Manufacturing |
| Engineers | Advanced industry |
| Investors | Administration (no workforce contribution) |

The guide specifically notes that investors do not work in production buildings despite generating the highest tax income. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Workforce Balance

Always monitor:

- Available workforce
- Required workforce

If available workforce becomes negative:

- production slows
- factories stop operating
- shortages spread through the economy

A stable workforce reserve is preferable to operating continuously at zero spare workers.

---

# Consumption

Every residence continuously consumes goods.

Consumption increases with:

- population size
- upgraded residences
- higher social classes

Luxury goods generally increase happiness and income without being required for promotion, while basic goods are necessary for progression.

---

# Basic Needs vs Luxury Needs

## Basic Needs

Required for:

- residence upgrades
- population growth
- stable progression

Examples include:

- fish
- bread
- clothing
- sausages
- coffee (higher tiers)

## Luxury Needs

Provide:

- additional income
- increased happiness
- greater tax revenue

Examples vary by population tier and include goods such as rum, beer, jewelry, gramophones, and chocolate. The guide details how luxury demands expand for engineers and investors. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# Production Costs

Every factory has hidden costs:

- workforce
- maintenance
- logistics
- warehouse traffic
- transportation
- opportunity cost

Sometimes importing a product is cheaper than manufacturing it locally.

---

# Warehousing

Warehouses do not generate income.

However, they directly influence productivity.

Poor warehouse placement causes:

- waiting carts
- delayed production
- wasted workforce
- reduced output

Warehouse congestion is often a larger bottleneck than insufficient production.

---

# Storage

Large stockpiles are useful only when:

- preparing expansion
- preparing expeditions
- constructing monuments
- buffering trade routes

Otherwise:

> Producing goods that are never consumed simply increases maintenance costs.

---

# Productivity

Every production building has a productivity value.

Increasing productivity generally means:

- more goods
- same workforce
- same footprint

Sources of productivity increases include:

- electricity
- specialist items
- working conditions
- cultural bonuses
- research
- DLC mechanics

The guide explains that specialist items equipped in Trade Unions and similar buildings can increase productivity, reduce workforce, lower maintenance, simplify production chains, and provide additional bonuses. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

# Working Conditions

Working conditions can temporarily increase productivity.

Advantages:

- more output
- fewer factories required

Disadvantages:

- reduced happiness
- increased riot risk

Use carefully, particularly during shortages.

---

# Influence

Influence becomes increasingly important in the late game.

It is spent on systems such as:

- ships
- trade routes
- cultural buildings
- expansions
- specialists

Investors significantly increase the amount of Influence available to your empire. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

# Trade

Trade generates wealth in several ways.

You may:

- sell surplus goods
- purchase missing resources
- establish automatic trade routes
- trade with NPCs
- import regional goods

As your empire expands into additional regions, trade transitions from optional to essential.

---

# Multi-Region Economy

Eventually your economy becomes global.

Typical flow:

```text
Old World
      │
      ├──────────────► New World
      │                     │
      │                     ▼
      │              Coffee
      │              Rum
      │              Cotton
      │              Gold
      │
      ▼
Advanced Goods
      │
      ▼
Investors
```

The source repeatedly highlights goods that must be imported between the Old World and New World, such as coffee, rum, tobacco, chocolate ingredients, rubber, pearls, and gold. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

# Financial Stability Checklist

A healthy economy usually has:

- ✅ Positive balance
- ✅ Spare workforce
- ✅ No production shortages
- ✅ Stable warehouse throughput
- ✅ Minimal idle factories
- ✅ Reliable trade routes
- ✅ Moderate stockpiles

---

# Warning Signs

⚠️ Constant negative income

⚠️ Empty warehouses

⚠️ Idle production chains

⚠️ Workforce shortages

⚠️ Excessive maintenance

⚠️ Goods permanently at maximum storage

⚠️ Continuous imports replacing domestic production

---

# Best Practices

- Expand gradually.
- Produce according to demand.
- Keep a workforce reserve.
- Avoid constructing expensive factories before they are needed.
- Optimize productivity before building duplicates.
- Use imports strategically to cover missing regional resources.
- Treat logistics as part of your economy, not as a separate system.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Population is the foundation of your economy.
- Needs drive production, and production drives income.
- Higher social classes are vastly more profitable but require increasingly complex supply chains.
- Efficient production is more valuable than simply producing more.
- A well-balanced economy scales naturally into the late game, whereas an oversized economy becomes expensive to maintain.
