# 07 — Cape Trelawney (Sunken Treasures DLC)

> **Cape Trelawney** is introduced in the **Sunken Treasures** DLC and serves as the ultimate region for building your primary metropolis. Unlike the Old World, it contains an enormous central island capable of supporting an industrial and residential city on a scale impossible elsewhere.

This chapter covers the strategic role of Cape Trelawney, when to expand into it, and how to develop it efficiently.

---

# Overview

Cape Trelawney is unlocked through the **Sunken Treasures** DLC.

After reaching approximately **700 Artisans**, Sir Archibald Blake requests your assistance in a story expedition. Completing this expedition unlocks the new region. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Unlike other regions, Cape Trelawney is set in an environment similar to the Old World.

---

# The Great Island

The defining feature of Cape Trelawney is its enormous central island.

Advantages include:

- Vast buildable area
- Numerous fertilities
- Large number of mineral deposits
- Space for monuments
- Ideal railway layouts
- Large industrial districts
- Massive residential neighborhoods

The guide specifically describes the region as containing an exceptionally large "continent island" with many fertilities and mineral deposits suitable for constructing a major city. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Purpose

Cape Trelawney is best viewed as your future:

- Capital
- Financial center
- Investor city
- Monument district
- Tourist destination
- Research center

Many players eventually relocate their primary city here.

---

# Expansion Timing

Do **not** rush into Cape Trelawney immediately after unlocking it.

Recommended conditions:

- Stable Old World economy
- Reliable trade routes
- Positive income
- Comfortable workforce reserves
- Steel production established
- Brick production established

A weak economy can struggle to support a second major city.

---

# Initial Settlement

Recommended priorities:

1. Trading Post
2. Warehouse
3. Timber production
4. Marketplace
5. Farmer housing
6. Basic food production

Treat the first settlement as you would a new Old World island.

---

# Long-Term Planning

Unlike smaller islands, the Great Island should be planned decades ahead.

Reserve districts for:

- Investors
- World's Fair
- Skyline projects
- Railways
- Cultural buildings
- Industrial complexes

Avoid placing temporary industries in prime residential locations.

---

# Recommended District Layout

```text
Harbor

↓

Industrial Zone

↓

Railway

↓

Engineers

↓

Investors

↓

Monuments

↓

Cultural District
```

Keeping these districts separated simplifies expansion and logistics.

---

# Industrial Advantages

Because of its enormous size, Cape Trelawney allows:

- centralized heavy industry
- large electricity grids
- efficient Trade Union coverage
- reduced shipping between production areas

Instead of splitting factories across multiple islands, many production chains can be consolidated here.

---

# Residential Planning

Large uninterrupted neighborhoods provide several benefits:

- easier public service coverage
- better Town Hall placement
- simplified road layouts
- room for future upgrades

The abundance of space allows city planning without many of the compromises required in the Old World.

---

# Railway Planning

The island is especially well suited to railway networks.

Plan railway corridors before dense residential construction begins.

Ideal approach:

```text
Oil Harbor

↓

Railway

↓

Power Plant

↓

Industrial District
```

Later expansion becomes significantly easier if railway space is reserved early.

---

# Trade Integration

Although self-sufficient in many respects, Cape Trelawney still depends on imported resources.

Typical imports include:

- Coffee
- Rum
- Rubber
- Cotton
- Cocoa
- Gold

These arrive primarily from the New World.

---

# Export Role

Cape Trelawney can also become a major exporter.

Possible exports:

- Steel
- Advanced machinery
- Luxury goods
- Construction materials

This reduces pressure on smaller Old World islands.

---

# Cultural District

The large buildable area makes Cape Trelawney an excellent location for:

- Zoo
- Botanical Garden
- Museum

Completing cultural collections increases attractiveness and provides powerful bonuses, as described later in the guide. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# Monuments

The spacious central island is ideal for monumental construction.

Typical projects include:

- World's Fair
- Large cultural complexes
- DLC monuments
- Skyline developments

Keeping monuments together also simplifies tourism planning.

---

# Population Strategy

Recommended order:

```text
Farmers

↓

Workers

↓

Artisans

↓

Engineers

↓

Investors
```

Avoid rushing directly to Investors before establishing reliable production.

---

# Logistics

Cape Trelawney often becomes the center of a global logistics network.

Typical flow:

```text
New World

↓

Cape Trelawney

↓

Finished Goods

↓

Other Regions
```

Its central role allows it to act as both producer and distribution hub.

---

# Optimization

Late-game optimization often focuses on:

- maximizing electricity coverage
- minimizing warehouse travel
- centralizing Trade Unions
- reducing shipping distances
- concentrating advanced industry

The island's size provides exceptional flexibility for redesigns.

---

# Common Mistakes

⚠️ Expanding too early.

⚠️ Treating the Great Island like a temporary colony.

⚠️ Ignoring future railway corridors.

⚠️ Scattering factories across the island.

⚠️ Filling valuable central land with low-tier housing.

⚠️ Building without a long-term city plan.

# Key Takeaways

- Cape Trelawney is designed to become the centerpiece of a mature empire.
- Its enormous central island removes many of the space constraints found in the Old World.
- Plan districts, railways, and monuments from the beginning to avoid expensive redesigns later.
- Use the region as a centralized industrial, financial, and cultural hub while relying on other regions for exclusive imported resources.
- Expansion into Cape Trelawney should strengthen an already stable empire, not rescue a struggling one.
