# 30 — Master Optimization Guide

> This chapter consolidates the optimization principles discussed throughout the source guide into a practical handbook. It focuses on how experienced players organize their empires to maximize efficiency, minimize bottlenecks, and scale into the late game.

> **Note:** This chapter is based on optimization concepts presented throughout the guide. It is intended as a strategic framework rather than a collection of exact building blueprints.

---

# The Philosophy of Optimization

The guide repeatedly reinforces one central principle:

> **A larger empire is not necessarily a better empire.**

Instead of constantly expanding production, improve what already exists.

The optimization cycle becomes:

```text
Analyze

↓

Identify Bottleneck

↓

Optimize

↓

Increase Output

↓

Repeat
```

---

# Industrial District Design

A mature industrial district should combine every major production bonus.

Ideal structure:

```text
Warehouse

+

Trade Union

+

Electricity

+

Related Factories

↓

Maximum Productivity
```

Every factory should ideally benefit from:

- Electricity
- Trade Union coverage
- Short warehouse distance

# Factory Clustering

Instead of spreading production:

```text
❌ Factory

Road

Factory

Road

Factory
```

Prefer:

```text
✔ Factory Factory Factory

Factory Trade Union Factory

Factory Factory Factory
```

Advantages:

- Better specialist coverage
- Easier logistics
- Higher density
- Simpler expansion

---

# Warehouse Placement

Warehouse distance directly affects production.

Ideal workflow:

```text
Factory

↓

Warehouse

↓

Harbor
```

Keep cart travel as short as possible.

If carts spend too much time travelling, factories eventually pause production.

---

# Electricity Planning

Electricity should be planned **before** large industrial districts are built.

Typical layout:

```text
Oil Harbor

↓

Railway

↓

Power Plant

↓

Factory District
```

Avoid constructing factories that will later require demolition to connect railways.

---

# Trade Union Strategy

Each Trade Union should support **one industrial specialization**.

Examples:

Steel District

- Furnace
- Steelworks
- Brass Smeltery

Food District

- Bakery
- Brewery
- Cannery

Luxury District

- Bicycle Factory
- Gramophone Factory
- Jewelry

Avoid mixing unrelated industries inside the same Trade Union radius.

---

# Residential Planning

Residential districts should prioritize:

- Town Hall coverage
- Public services
- Future expansion
- Attractive surroundings

Typical arrangement:

```text
Town Hall

↓

Engineer Blocks

↓

Investor Blocks

↓

Parks

↓

Museum
```

Dense housing maximizes Town Hall value.

# Logistics Optimization

Every production chain depends on logistics.

Ideal flow:

```text
Raw Materials

↓

Cargo Ship

↓

Factory

↓

Warehouse

↓

Consumer
```

Transport delays eventually reduce factory utilization.

---

# Harbor Design

Busy harbors should include:

- Multiple piers
- Large depots
- Harbormaster coverage
- Expansion space

Congested harbors become one of the largest late-game bottlenecks.

---

# Trade Route Optimization

Guidelines:

✔ Full cargo

✔ Dedicated routes

✔ Reliable scheduling

✔ Minimal waiting

Avoid:

- Mixed cargo where unnecessary
- Long chains of intermediate stops
- Empty return journeys

---

# Workforce Optimization

Goal:

```text
Minimum Workforce

↓

Maximum Production
```

Achieve this through:

- Specialists
- Productivity bonuses
- Electricity
- Alternative recipes

Rather than simply increasing population.

# Research Optimization

Late-game priorities:

1. Duplicate powerful specialists.
2. Improve major production chains.
3. Optimize logistics.
4. Expand optimization to additional islands.

Research should always target systems with the greatest empire-wide impact.

# Expansion vs Optimization

Whenever you encounter a shortage, ask:

```text
Can I optimize?

↓

YES

↓

Optimize
```

Only expand production if optimization cannot solve the problem.

This principle appears repeatedly throughout the guide.

---

# Common Bottlenecks

Most frequent causes of inefficiency:

1. Long warehouse distances
2. Missing electricity
3. Poor Trade Union coverage
4. Harbor congestion
5. Weak logistics
6. Missing imports
7. Workforce shortages

Always identify the true bottleneck before constructing additional factories.

# The Optimization Loop

A fully developed empire continuously follows this cycle:

```text
Identify Bottleneck

↓

Optimize Existing Production

↓

Increase Efficiency

↓

Support More Population

↓

Generate More Income

↓

Invest in Research

↓

Duplicate Better Specialists

↓

Optimize Again
```

Rather than pursuing endless territorial expansion, the guide emphasizes **continuous refinement** of every interconnected system.

---

# Master Principles

1. Optimize before expanding.
2. Specialize every island.
3. Keep logistics simple.
4. Electrify all advanced industry.
5. Cluster factories around Trade Unions.
6. Build dense residential districts around Town Halls.
7. Use research to remove randomness.
8. Complete valuable collections.
9. Eliminate bottlenecks systematically.
10. Think of the empire as **one interconnected production machine**, not a collection of independent islands.

---

# Key Takeaways

- The strongest empires are built through optimization rather than unchecked expansion.
- Electricity, specialists, and research multiply the effectiveness of every production chain.
- Specialized islands and efficient logistics outperform self-sufficient settlements.
- Regular bottleneck analysis is the key to continuous improvement.
- Endgame success comes from refining every system until the entire empire operates as a single, highly efficient network.
