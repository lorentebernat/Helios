# 09 — Enbesa (Land of Lions DLC)

> **Enbesa**, introduced in the **Land of Lions** DLC, is a unique region focused on irrigation, agriculture, and scientific progress. Unlike the Old World or the Arctic, its economy revolves around fertile river systems and eventually unlocks one of the most powerful late-game mechanics in Anno 1800: the **Research Institute**.

This chapter summarizes the mechanics and progression presented in the source guide.

---

# Overview

Enbesa becomes available through the **Land of Lions** DLC.

Unlike previous regions:

- agriculture depends on irrigation
- water management is the central mechanic
- unique population classes exist
- exclusive resources are produced
- scientific research becomes possible later in the game

The guide introduces Enbesa as a separate region with its own progression and eventually connects it to the Research Institute and Scholar population. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# Population

Enbesa contains two primary population classes.

```text
Shepherds

↓

Elders
```

Later in the overall game progression, **Scholars** become available in the Old World after completing the Enbesa storyline.

Scholars are technically an Old World population tier but are directly linked to the Land of Lions expansion. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Regional Identity

Enbesa is characterized by:

- dry climate
- river valleys
- irrigation canals
- agricultural specialization
- cultural development
- scientific progression

Unlike the industrial focus of the Old World, Enbesa emphasizes careful land management.

---

# Irrigation

Irrigation is the defining mechanic of the region.

Basic concept:

```text
River

↓

Canal

↓

Irrigated Land

↓

Farm Production
```

Many farms cannot operate unless connected to an irrigation network.

The guide presents irrigation canals as essential infrastructure for agricultural production in Enbesa. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# Canal Planning

Effective irrigation requires planning.

Goals:

- maximize irrigated farmland
- minimize canal length
- avoid wasted water coverage
- reserve land for future expansion

Poor irrigation layouts significantly reduce productivity.

---

# Agriculture

Most production revolves around irrigated farms.

Typical regional products include:

- Teff
- Hibiscus
- Indigo
- Clay
- Regional livestock products

These resources support both local consumption and exports to other regions. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

# Building Strategy

Unlike the Old World:

- farms dominate the landscape
- irrigation determines settlement placement
- production districts follow water access rather than roads

Planning begins with the river, not the city.

---

# Shepherds

Shepherds are the entry-level population.

They unlock:

- basic agriculture
- irrigation infrastructure
- early production chains
- regional construction

Their economy focuses on food production and essential goods.

---

# Elders

Elders unlock more advanced production.

Additional industries include:

- luxury goods
- regional services
- cultural development
- exports

As with every population promotion, additional needs must be supplied before advancement.

---

# Regional Production

Unlike the Old World, Enbesa emphasizes long agricultural chains instead of heavy industry.

Example structure:

```text
Farm

↓

Processing

↓

Finished Good

↓

Population
```

Heavy manufacturing remains concentrated in the Old World.

---

# Trade Role

Enbesa supports the wider empire by exporting unique resources.

Typical exports include:

- Hibiscus
- Teff
- Indigo
- Regional luxury goods

These goods later become important for Scholars and advanced Old World production.

---

# Logistics

Efficient Enbesa logistics require balancing:

- irrigation
- warehouse access
- export harbors
- canal placement

Unlike other regions, transportation is often secondary to water management.

---

# Story Progression

The Land of Lions DLC contains an extensive narrative campaign.

Completing it unlocks several advanced mechanics, most importantly:

- Scholars
- Research Institute
- Scientific research

The guide ties these systems directly to successful progression through Enbesa. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

# Scholars

Although Scholars live in the Old World, they depend on Enbesa.

Scholars provide:

- Research Points
- access to advanced research
- item creation
- technological improvements

They become one of the strongest late-game population classes.

The guide notes that Enbesa-related museum collections can even increase research output from Scholar residences. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

# Research Institute

The Research Institute fundamentally changes the late game.

It allows players to:

- develop technologies
- duplicate specialist items
- unlock unique bonuses
- accelerate optimization

This transforms many rare items from one-time rewards into reproducible assets.

---

# Optimization

Efficient Enbesa settlements usually feature:

- centralized irrigation
- compact villages
- specialized agricultural districts
- export-focused logistics

Avoid overbuilding heavy industry within the region.

---

# Common Mistakes

⚠️ Designing farms before planning irrigation.

⚠️ Wasting irrigated land.

⚠️ Expanding settlements too quickly.

⚠️ Ignoring export logistics.

⚠️ Treating Enbesa like another Old World region.

⚠️ Delaying the storyline that unlocks Scholars.

# Key Takeaways

- Enbesa is centered on irrigation rather than industrialization.
- Water management is the region's defining mechanic.
- Complete the regional storyline to unlock Scholars and the Research Institute.
- Treat Enbesa as a specialized agricultural and scientific support region.
- Its long-term importance lies in enabling powerful late-game optimization rather than generating large tax income.
