# 05 — The Old World

> The **Old World** is the heart of every Anno 1800 game. It is where your empire begins, where your primary population develops, and where most industrialization takes place.

This chapter covers the structure, strengths, limitations, and long-term planning of the Old World.

---

# Overview

The Old World is your primary economic center.

It contains:

- Farmers
- Workers
- Artisans
- Engineers
- Investors

Most advanced industries, monuments, and financial power originate here. The guide treats the Old World as the central region from which expansion into the New World and DLC regions begins. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# Role Within Your Empire

Think of the Old World as your:

- Administrative capital
- Industrial center
- Financial center
- Research hub
- Monument district

Later regions mainly support it by supplying resources unavailable locally.

---

# Population Progression

```text
Farmers

↓

Workers

↓

Artisans

↓

Engineers

↓

Investors
```

Each promotion unlocks new buildings, production chains, and public services while increasing consumption requirements. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Settlement Priorities

A successful island usually develops in this order:

1. Harbor
2. Marketplace
3. Housing
4. Timber
5. Food
6. Clothing
7. Expansion

Only after these foundations are stable should higher-tier production be introduced.

---

# Island Evaluation

Before settling an island, consider:

## Fertilities

Examples include:

- Grain
- Hops
- Potatoes
- Peppers
- Grapes

These determine which agricultural chains the island can support.

---

## Mineral Deposits

Look for:

- Iron
- Coal
- Clay
- Oil (later)
- Limestone
- Gold (where available)

Mining capacity strongly influences the island's industrial potential.

---

## Coastline

Long coastlines provide space for:

- Harbors
- Shipyards
- Fisheries
- Defensive structures

---

## Buildable Area

Flat, uninterrupted terrain is generally preferable for:

- large cities
- industrial districts
- monuments
- railway networks

---

# Fertility Strategy

No island possesses every fertility.

Instead of searching endlessly for the "perfect" island:

- specialize islands
- connect them with trade routes
- centralize advanced industry

This is one of the game's intended logistics challenges.

---

# Capital Island

Most empires eventually develop one primary capital.

Typical characteristics:

- highest population
- investors
- monuments
- cultural buildings
- banks
- electricity
- advanced industry

---

# Specialized Islands

Rather than duplicating every production chain everywhere, create specialist islands.

Examples:

## Agricultural Island

Produces:

- Grain
- Potatoes
- Hops
- Peppers

Exports finished or intermediate goods.

---

## Industrial Island

Produces:

- Steel
- Brass
- Steam Motors
- Heavy machinery

Usually powered entirely by electricity.

---

## Mining Island

Focuses on:

- Iron
- Coal
- Limestone
- Oil

Often has relatively little residential development.

---

# City Planning

A practical city is usually divided into districts.

```text
Residential

↓

Commercial

↓

Public Services

↓

Industrial

↓

Harbor
```

Separating residential and industrial zones reduces congestion and simplifies future expansion.

---

# Residential Districts

Keep housing close to:

- Marketplace
- Fire Station
- Police
- School
- Hospital

Later:

- Bank
- Electricity (indirectly through nearby industry and services)
- Tourist infrastructure (if applicable)

---

# Industrial Districts

Heavy industry should be grouped together.

Benefits include:

- shared warehouses
- electricity coverage
- Trade Union coverage
- simplified logistics

Avoid scattering factories throughout residential neighborhoods.

---

# Harbor District

The harbor eventually becomes one of the busiest parts of the island.

Typical buildings include:

- Trading Post
- Depots
- Piers
- Repair Crane
- Harbourmaster's Office
- Shipyards

Later DLCs further expand harbor management with additional mechanics and buildings. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

# Industrialization

The arrival of Engineers marks the beginning of true industrialization.

Major developments include:

- Electricity
- Oil
- Railways
- Steam Ships
- Advanced Manufacturing

From this point onward, productivity becomes more important than expansion alone.

---

# Electricity Network

Industrial areas should be planned around power plants.

Example:

```text
Power Plant

├──────── Steel

├──────── Brass

├──────── Steam Motors

├──────── Concrete

└──────── Heavy Industry
```

Factories outside the coverage area lose significant efficiency.

---

# Trade Integration

The Old World eventually imports many essential goods.

Examples include:

- Coffee
- Rum
- Cotton
- Gold
- Rubber
- Chocolate ingredients

Most of these originate in the New World or other expansion regions. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

# Endgame Development

Late-game Old World cities focus less on expansion and more on optimization.

Typical objectives:

- maximize investor population
- increase attractiveness
- optimize production
- reduce maintenance
- improve logistics
- complete cultural collections
- construct monuments

---

# Common Mistakes

⚠️ Trying to make every island self-sufficient.

⚠️ Building heavy industry inside residential districts.

⚠️ Ignoring future railway placement.

⚠️ Expanding population faster than production.

⚠️ Choosing islands without considering long-term resources.

⚠️ Delaying inter-island trade.

# Key Takeaways

- The Old World is the foundation of your empire.
- Plan your capital for long-term growth rather than short-term convenience.
- Specialize supporting islands instead of duplicating every production chain.
- Keep industry, logistics, and residential areas organized into distinct districts.
- Prepare early for electricity, railways, and interregional trade, as these systems become central to late-game success.
