# 06 — The New World

> The **New World** is the first expansion beyond the Old World and forms the backbone of your late-game economy. While its population is simpler, it produces many strategic resources that are impossible to obtain elsewhere.

This chapter explains the purpose of the New World, its unique population, economy, logistics, and long-term integration into your empire.

---

# Overview

The New World is **not** intended to replace the Old World.

Instead, it exists to support it.

Its primary role is to produce exclusive resources required by higher-tier Old World citizens.

The source guide introduces the New World as a separate region with its own population tiers, exclusive fertilities, and production chains that feed the Old World economy. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# Population

Unlike the Old World, the New World has only two population classes.

```text
Jornaleros

↓

Obreros
```

These populations unlock regional production buildings and support the export economy.

Although their progression is simpler, their production is essential for Engineers and Investors.

---

# Purpose

The New World specializes in:

- Tropical agriculture
- Plantation crops
- Precious resources
- Luxury goods
- Industrial raw materials

Almost everything produced here is eventually exported.

---

# Core Economic Loop

```text
New World

↓

Raw Materials

↓

Export

↓

Old World

↓

Luxury Goods

↓

Engineers

↓

Investors
```

This interregional dependency is one of the defining mechanics of the mid and late game.

---

# Exclusive Resources

The guide identifies several resources that originate exclusively in the New World.

Examples include:

- Coffee
- Cotton
- Sugar Cane
- Tobacco
- Cocoa
- Rubber
- Bananas
- Pearls
- Gold Ore

Many advanced Old World production chains cannot operate without these imports. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Plantation Economy

Most agricultural production is plantation-based.

Typical plantations include:

- Coffee
- Sugar Cane
- Tobacco
- Cocoa
- Cotton
- Rubber

Plantations occupy large areas and usually become the dominant land use on specialized islands.

---

# Export Philosophy

Unlike the Old World, the New World rarely consumes its own high-value production.

Instead:

```text
Coffee

↓

Ship

↓

Old World

↓

Engineers
```

or

```text
Rubber

↓

Ship

↓

Advanced Industry
```

Efficient export logistics are therefore more important than maximizing local population.

---

# Island Specialization

Specialization is even more effective here than in the Old World.

Example:

### Coffee Island

- Coffee plantations
- Warehouses
- Harbor

---

### Sugar Island

- Sugar plantations
- Rum production (if desired)
- Export docks

---

### Rubber Island

- Rubber plantations
- Minimal housing
- Dedicated transport fleet

---

# Fertility

Every island has different plantation fertilities.

No island supports every crop.

Expansion should therefore focus on obtaining:

- missing fertilities
- additional buildable land
- strategic mineral deposits

Trade routes then connect these specialized islands.

---

# Mining

Although agriculture dominates the region, mining also plays an important role.

Important resources include:

- Gold
- Oil (later expansion)
- Mineral deposits depending on island generation

Gold is particularly valuable because it supports several advanced luxury production chains.

---

# Housing

Residential layouts remain relatively straightforward.

Typical services include:

- Market
- Fire protection
- Basic public services

Compared with the Old World, city planning is generally less complex.

---

# Logistics

The New World is fundamentally an export economy.

Most logistics revolve around:

- transporting plantation products
- maintaining regular shipping
- supplying construction materials
- importing advanced goods when necessary

---

# Harbour Planning

Every major production island should include:

- Trading Post
- Depots
- Warehouses
- Loading facilities

Export efficiency is more valuable than decorative city planning.

# Expansion Strategy

A recommended order:

1. Secure Coffee.
2. Secure Cotton.
3. Secure Sugar Cane.
4. Secure Tobacco.
5. Secure Cocoa.
6. Expand Rubber production.
7. Increase Gold production.

Exact priorities may vary depending on your current Old World population and production needs.

---

# Workforce

The New World workforce is generally easier to manage.

Most shortages are solved by:

- upgrading residences
- expanding housing
- improving supply chains

Unlike the Old World, population progression is shorter and more specialized.

---

# Trade Routes

Successful New World logistics rely on dedicated shipping.

Typical routes include:

```text
Coffee

Island A

↓

Capital
```

```text
Cotton

Island B

↓

Industrial Island
```

```text
Rubber

Island C

↓

Engineers
```

Avoid combining too many unrelated goods onto a single trade route if it complicates inventory management.

---

# Optimization

As your empire grows:

- centralize plantation districts
- minimize warehouse travel distances
- maximize ship utilization
- specialize islands
- avoid unnecessary local industry

The New World's greatest strength lies in producing raw materials efficiently, not in becoming a second Old World.

---

# Common Mistakes

⚠️ Attempting to make every island self-sufficient.

⚠️ Delaying Coffee production.

⚠️ Ignoring Rubber until demand becomes critical.

⚠️ Mixing unrelated plantation industries on the same island without planning.

⚠️ Using too few cargo ships for exports.

⚠️ Expanding population instead of improving logistics.

---

# Key Takeaways

- The New World exists to supply resources that the Old World cannot produce.
- Plantation agriculture is the region's defining feature.
- Efficient exports are more important than building large cities.
- Specialization and reliable trade routes are the foundation of a successful New World economy.
- As Engineers and Investors grow, the importance of New World production increases dramatically.
