# 01 — Game Progression

> This chapter explains how a typical game of **Anno 1800** progresses from a small settlement to a global industrial empire. It focuses on the natural order in which mechanics, population tiers, and regions become available.

---

# Overview

Anno 1800 is a progression-based city builder.

Every advancement is driven by one fundamental concept:

> **Meet the needs of your current population to unlock the next population tier.**

Each new tier unlocks:

- new buildings
- new production chains
- new services
- new construction materials
- new technologies
- new transportation methods
- increasingly profitable taxation

At the same time, every promotion also introduces new demands that increase the complexity of your economy. This progression—from lower to higher population tiers, each unlocking additional mechanics and production—is the organizing principle of the source guide. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

# Stage 1 — Establishing Your Colony

## Primary Objectives

- Secure an island.
- Build a Marketplace.
- Construct roads.
- Provide housing.
- Produce timber.
- Produce fish.
- Produce work clothes.

At this stage, expansion should remain controlled. Large stockpiles of unused goods generally provide little benefit compared to maintaining stable production.

---

# Stage 2 — Farmers

## Goals

Build a functioning village.

### Priorities

- Food
- Clothing
- Marketplace coverage
- Fire protection
- Stable income

### Unlock Focus

- Basic agriculture
- Simple production
- Initial workforce

Avoid expanding faster than your production can support.

---

# Stage 3 — Workers

Workers represent the first significant increase in production capability.

## New priorities

- Bricks
- Steel
- Soap
- Bread
- Schools
- Police

Production chains become noticeably longer than during the Farmer stage, requiring more careful planning and logistics. The guide introduces additional public services and more advanced manufacturing as workers are unlocked. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Stage 4 — Artisans

Artisans are the first population tier that significantly changes gameplay.

## Major unlocks

- Canned Food
- Sewing Machines
- Fur Coats
- Rum consumption
- University
- Shipyard improvements

At this point the player should begin thinking about expansion beyond the starting island.

Some important resources cannot be produced everywhere due to fertility limitations.

---

# Expansion Begins

Once artisans are established, resource shortages become common.

Typical reasons to colonize additional islands include:

- missing fertilities
- mineral deposits
- additional workforce
- future expansion space

Trade routes become an essential part of the economy rather than an optional convenience.

---

# Stage 5 — Engineers

Engineers introduce industrialization.

## Major unlocks

- Electricity
- Railways
- Oil
- Banks
- Steam Shipyard
- Advanced factories

Many high-end production buildings now require electricity to operate efficiently, and new luxury goods become available. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Industrial production becomes significantly more efficient once powered.

---

# Electricity Changes Everything

Electricity is one of the most important milestones in the game.

Benefits include:

- higher productivity
- faster production
- access to advanced factories
- larger industrial capacity

Because power plants have limited range, industrial districts benefit from centralized planning.

---

# Steam Age

With engineers, the economy expands dramatically.

Typical developments include:

- Steam ships
- Oil logistics
- Advanced industry
- Increased trade
- Large-scale manufacturing

The focus shifts from survival to optimization.

---

# Stage 6 — Investors

Investors are the highest population tier in the Old World.

They:

- generate the highest tax income
- increase Influence
- unlock monuments
- demand numerous luxury goods
- do not contribute workforce

The guide also notes that investors have unique consumption patterns compared with engineers and unlock the World's Fair, additional luxury goods, and later-game production chains. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

# World's Fair

One of the largest late-game projects.

Characteristics include:

- constructed in multiple stages
- consumes large quantities of resources
- requires Investors
- rewards unique items
- increases attractiveness

Different exhibition themes provide different categories of rewards. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

# New Regions

As progression continues, additional regions become available.

These introduce:

- new populations
- exclusive resources
- unique production chains
- regional mechanics

Examples described in the source include:

- New World
- Cape Trelawney
- Arctic
- Enbesa

Each region specializes in producing goods unavailable elsewhere, encouraging interregional trade. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

# Late Game

The focus gradually shifts from expansion to optimization.

Typical goals become:

- maximizing productivity
- reducing maintenance
- optimizing logistics
- completing cultural collections
- researching specialists
- building monuments
- increasing attractiveness
- improving tourism
- managing multiple regions

---

# Common Beginner Mistakes

⚠️ Expanding too quickly.

⚠️ Upgrading houses before production is ready.

⚠️ Ignoring warehouse capacity.

⚠️ Producing too many unnecessary goods.

⚠️ Neglecting trade routes.

⚠️ Building inefficient production layouts.

⚠️ Colonizing islands without a long-term purpose.

---

- [ ] Colonize second island
- [ ] Create first trade route
- [ ] Secure missing fertilities
- [ ] Expand workforce

---

## Industrial Phase

- [ ] Engineers unlocked
- [ ] Oil imported
- [ ] Electricity operating
- [ ] Steam ships available
- [ ] Industrial district established

---

## End Game

- [ ] Investors established
- [ ] World's Fair constructed
- [ ] Multiple regions connected
- [ ] Advanced logistics operational
- [ ] Production optimized
- [ ] Cultural collections progressing

---

# Key Takeaways

- Always satisfy current needs before upgrading residences.
- Expand production to match demand rather than creating large surpluses.
- Treat additional islands as specialized suppliers.
- Plan industrial districts with future electricity coverage in mind.
- Expect complexity to increase substantially with each population tier and new region.
