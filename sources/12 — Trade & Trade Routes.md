# 12 — Trade & Trade Routes

> Trade is the system that transforms individual islands into a global empire. While early settlements can survive independently, the mid and late game depend almost entirely on efficient transportation of goods between islands and regions.

This chapter explains how trade works, how to design effective trade routes, and how to optimize your logistics network.

---

# Overview

Trade serves four primary purposes:

- Supply population needs
- Move raw materials
- Support production chains
- Balance regional economies

As additional regions become available, trade shifts from being optional to becoming one of the game's most important mechanics. The source guide introduces increasing dependence on interregional transport as New World and DLC regions unlock. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# Trade Progression

Typical development:

```text
Single Island

↓

Multiple Islands

↓

Regional Trade

↓

Global Trade Network
```

Each stage introduces greater logistical complexity.

---

# Why Trade Exists

No single island contains:

- every fertility
- every mineral deposit
- unlimited building space

Trade compensates for these natural limitations.

Instead of building everything everywhere:

- specialize islands
- transport goods
- centralize advanced industry

---

# Trade Route Structure

Every route follows the same pattern.

```text
Loading Harbor

↓

Cargo Ship

↓

Destination Harbor

↓

Warehouse

↓

Consumption
```

The objective is to maintain continuous movement without shortages or unnecessary stockpiles.

# Island Specialization

Efficient empires rarely produce every good everywhere.

Example:

```text
Island A

Agriculture

↓

Island B

↓

```

- easier optimization
- better specialist coverage

# Cargo Ships

Cargo ships transport goods between islands.

Typical roles include:

- Local transport
- Regional supply
- Intercontinental shipping

The guide later introduces steam-powered cargo ships as major upgrades over sailing vessels. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# Steam Ships

Advantages include:

- higher speed
- greater cargo capacity
- improved reliability

Steam cargo ships become increasingly valuable as shipping distances grow.

---

# Airships

With the Arctic DLC, airships provide another transportation method.

Advantages:

- bypass naval routes
- reach remote Arctic settlements
- rapid delivery

Disadvantages:

- limited cargo
- expensive construction

Air logistics complement rather than replace naval transport. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

# Harbor Infrastructure

Efficient trade depends on well-developed harbors.

Important buildings include:

- Trading Post
- Depots
- Piers
- Repair Crane
- Harbormaster's Office

Large trade hubs require sufficient loading capacity to prevent congestion.

---

# Loading Efficiency

Every ship spends time:

- loading
- unloading
- waiting

Reduce idle time by:

- increasing pier capacity
- minimizing unnecessary stops
- separating busy routes

---

# Balanced Routes

A good route transports cargo in both directions.

Example:

```text
Old World

↓

Construction Materials

↓

New World

↓

Coffee

↓

Old World
```

Ships returning empty represent lost efficiency.

---

# Storage Management

Trade routes should maintain stable inventories.

Avoid:

- overflowing warehouses
- permanent shortages
- excessive stockpiles

Monitor island storage regularly and adjust cargo quantities as demand changes.

---

# Emergency Supply

Some routes exist primarily as backups.

Typical emergency cargo:

- Timber
- Steel Beams
- Bricks
- Food
- Coal

These routes allow rapid expansion and recovery after disasters.

---

# Trade with NPCs

In addition to your own routes, the guide describes trading with AI-controlled characters.

Benefits include:

- purchasing rare goods
- selling surpluses
- acquiring resources temporarily

NPC trade is useful for filling short-term shortages but is generally less efficient than internal production.

---

# Harbor Specialists

Harbormaster's Offices can improve nearby harbor buildings.

Possible bonuses include:

- faster loading
- additional goods
- reduced maintenance
- improved repair speed
- stronger defenses

Specialist items significantly increase harbor efficiency. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

# Common Trade Problems

## Chronic Shortages

Possible causes:

- insufficient production
- overloaded ships
- warehouse congestion
- route frequency too low

---

## Overflowing Warehouses

Possible causes:

- overproduction
- excessive imports
- insufficient consumption

---

## Idle Ships

Possible causes:

- inefficient scheduling
- too many stops
- unnecessary waiting

---

# Trade Network Evolution

A mature empire often develops a hierarchy.

```text
Regional Producer

↓

Regional Distribution Hub

↓

Capital

↓

Population
```

This reduces duplicate shipping and simplifies logistics.

---

# Common Mistakes

⚠️ Trying to make every island self-sufficient.

⚠️ Creating overly complex trade routes.

⚠️ Ignoring harbor congestion.

⚠️ Allowing ships to travel empty.

⚠️ Mixing unrelated cargo on busy routes.

⚠️ Expanding production without increasing transport capacity.

---

# Trade Checklist

## Every Trade Route Should

- [ ] Have a clear purpose.
- [ ] Carry useful cargo.
- [ ] Avoid unnecessary stops.
- [ ] Maintain stable inventories.

---

## Every Harbor Should

- [ ] Have adequate storage.
- [ ] Have enough loading capacity.
- [ ] Support future expansion.

---

## Every Region Should

- [ ] Export its unique resources.
- [ ] Import required construction materials.
- [ ] Remain connected to the empire.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Trade is the backbone of a multi-island empire.
- Specialization is more efficient than self-sufficiency.
- Reliable trade routes are essential for supporting Engineers, Investors, and DLC regions.
- Harbor infrastructure and cargo management are just as important as production.
- As your empire grows, optimizing transportation often yields greater benefits than constructing additional factories.
