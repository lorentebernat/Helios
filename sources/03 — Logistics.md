# 03 — Logistics

> Logistics is the invisible engine that powers every successful empire in **Anno 1800**. Efficient production means little if goods cannot move quickly between factories, warehouses, islands, and regions.

---

# Overview

Every product follows the same basic lifecycle:

```text
Raw Resource
      │
      ▼
Processing
      │
      ▼
Intermediate Goods
      │
      ▼
Finished Goods
      │
      ▼
Warehouse
      │
      ▼
Population / Factory
      │
      ▼
Consumption
```

Every interruption along this path reduces the overall efficiency of your economy.

---

# Logistics Priorities

As your empire grows, logistics gradually become more important than production itself.

A mature economy spends more effort on:

- moving goods
- reducing transport times
- balancing inventories
- avoiding bottlenecks
- optimizing shipping

rather than simply building more factories.

---

# Transportation Levels

There are several transportation layers.

```text
Factory

↓

Warehouse

↓

Harbour

↓

Trade Route

↓

Destination Harbour

↓

Warehouse

↓

Factory / Population
```

Each layer can become a bottleneck independently.

---

# Roads

Roads determine how quickly carts transport goods.

Every production building sends carts to the nearest warehouse.

Poor road layouts increase:

- travel time
- warehouse congestion
- idle factories

Good layouts reduce transportation delays without requiring additional production.

---

# Road Hierarchy

Typical progression:

```text
Dirt Roads

↓

Paved Roads
```

Paved roads increase transportation speed and become essential in large industrial cities.

The New World and Arctic introduce regional variations, but the same principles apply. The guide notes that paved roads unlock with higher population tiers in the New World, while Arctic settlements use snow roads adapted to that region. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0} :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Warehouses

Warehouses are the center of every production district.

Their responsibilities include:

- receiving goods
- distributing goods
- storing inventory
- servicing production buildings

Factories stop working whenever warehouse access becomes saturated.

---

# Warehouse Coverage

Ideal layout:

```text
Factory

Factory

Warehouse

Factory

Factory
```

Goals:

- short travel distance
- minimal intersections
- balanced traffic

---

# Warehouse Congestion

Symptoms include:

- carts waiting
- idle factories
- overflowing storage
- inconsistent production

Solutions:

- upgrade warehouses
- build additional warehouses
- shorten road distances
- separate industrial districts

---

# Harbour Infrastructure

Every island begins with a trading post.

Later expansions include:

- depots
- piers
- repair cranes
- harbourmaster offices
- defensive structures

Harbour infrastructure becomes increasingly important as international trade expands. The guide also describes Harbourmaster Offices, which improve nearby coastal buildings through specialist items. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# Storage Capacity

Storage is shared across the island.

Increasing storage allows:

- larger imports
- export reserves
- construction stockpiles
- production buffering

However:

Large storage does **not** increase production efficiency by itself.

---

# Island Specialization

One of the most effective logistics strategies is specialization.

Example:

```text
Island A

Agriculture

↓

Island B

Heavy Industry

↓

Island C

Population
```

Advantages:

- simpler layouts
- easier optimization
- fewer warehouse conflicts
- centralized production

---

# Fertility Logistics

Every island has different fertilities.

Example:

```text
Island 1

Wheat

↓

Bread

↓

Export

────────────►

Capital
```

Later regions introduce unique fertilities that require regular transport back to the Old World, including crops and industrial resources unavailable elsewhere. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

# Mineral Logistics

Many deposits are limited.

Typical examples:

- Iron
- Coal
- Gold
- Oil
- Gas (Arctic)

Rather than forcing every island to be self-sufficient, transport these resources where they are needed.

---

# Inter-Regional Logistics

Eventually your empire spans multiple regions.

Typical trade network:

```text
Old World

▲

│

Coffee

Rum

Gold

Cotton

Chocolate

▼

New World
```

Additional regions later introduce their own exclusive goods, creating a global logistics network. The guide repeatedly identifies resources that must be imported between regions to satisfy higher-tier populations. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

# Trade Routes

Trade routes automate transportation.

Every route should answer three questions:

- What is being transported?
- Where is it going?
- How often is it needed?

Well-designed routes are predictable, simple, and easy to expand.

---

# Cargo Balance

Avoid ships that:

- travel half empty
- wait unnecessarily
- transport tiny quantities

Whenever possible:

```text
Export

↓

Unload

↓

Reload

↓

Return
```

Ships should carry useful cargo in both directions whenever practical.

---

# Fleet Roles

Different ships serve different logistical purposes.

Typical categories:

- Small local transport
- Regional cargo
- Long-distance cargo
- Oil transport
- Air transport (DLC)

The guide distinguishes sailing cargo ships, steam cargo ships, specialized oil tankers, and later DLC airships, each with different capabilities and purposes. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

# Industrial Districts

Factories with similar inputs should be grouped.

Example:

```text
Steel

↓

Weapons

↓

Steam Motors

↓

Heavy Industry
```

Benefits:

- shared warehouses
- easier electricity coverage
- shorter transport paths

---

# Agricultural Districts

Agriculture requires large areas.

Keep farms together to:

- simplify road networks
- reduce travel time
- maximize available space

---

# Harbourmaster Optimization

Harbourmaster Offices affect nearby coastal buildings.

Specialist items can provide bonuses such as:

- increased productivity
- reduced maintenance
- faster loading
- additional goods
- improved repair speed
- stronger harbor defenses

These effects apply only within the building's influence radius. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

---

# Late-Game Logistics

Late-game logistics revolve around efficiency rather than expansion.

Typical objectives:

- eliminate bottlenecks
- maximize ship utilization
- centralize industry
- reduce unnecessary transportation
- stabilize inventories

---

# Common Logistics Mistakes

⚠️ Factories too far from warehouses.

⚠️ Long, winding road networks.

⚠️ One warehouse serving an entire industrial district.

⚠️ Excessive imports of basic goods.

⚠️ Ships transporting very small loads.

⚠️ Producing every good on every island.

⚠️ Ignoring regional specialization.

---

# Logistics Checklist

## Every Production Area Should Have

- [ ] Nearby warehouse
- [ ] Direct road access
- [ ] Expansion space
- [ ] Minimal traffic crossings

---

## Every Island Should Have

- [ ] Adequate storage
- [ ] Efficient harbour
- [ ] Reliable imports
- [ ] Reliable exports

---

## Every Trade Route Should

- [ ] Transport essential goods
- [ ] Avoid unnecessary stops
- [ ] Keep ships busy
- [ ] Maintain stable inventories

---

# Key Takeaways

- Logistics is the foundation of every production chain.
- Efficient transportation is often more valuable than additional factories.
- Specialize islands instead of making each one self-sufficient.
- Warehouses, harbours, and trade routes should be designed together.
- As your empire grows, logistics become the primary limiting factor in economic performance.
