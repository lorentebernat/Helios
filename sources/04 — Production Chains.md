# 04 — Production Chains

> Production chains are the heart of **Anno 1800**. Every manufactured product depends on one or more previous goods, creating an increasingly complex network as your population advances.

This chapter explains how production chains work, how to optimize them, and how to organize your industrial districts efficiently.

---

# Production Philosophy

Every production chain follows the same principle:

```text
Natural Resource

↓

Raw Material

↓

Intermediate Product

↓

Finished Product

↓

Population Consumption
```

The further you progress through the game, the more intermediate products are required before reaching the final good.

# Chain Categories

Production can be divided into several major categories.

## Agriculture

Produces crops such as:

- Grain
- Potatoes
- Hops
- Peppers
- Grapes

These buildings generally require large field areas.

---

## Animal Farming

Produces:

- Wool
- Milk
- Beef
- Pigs

Animal farms also require pasture space.

---

## Fishing

Produces seafood directly from coastal buildings.

Examples include:

- Fish
- Pearls (New World)
- Whales (Arctic DLC)

Regional fishing industries expand significantly in DLC regions. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## Forestry

Produces:

- Timber
- Charcoal
- Hunting products

These buildings require nearby forest coverage.

---

## Mining

Extracts finite resources.

Typical examples:

- Iron
- Coal
- Gold
- Zinc
- Copper
- Oil
- Gas

Some deposits exist only in specific regions.

---

## Heavy Industry

Consumes multiple raw materials.

Produces:

- Steel
- Brass
- Concrete
- Steam Motors
- Advanced machinery

These buildings become much more efficient when powered by electricity.

---

## Luxury Goods

Luxury production chains are among the most complicated.

Examples include:

- Chocolate
- Gramophones
- Pocket Watches
- Jewelry
- Champagne

These goods are generally consumed by higher population tiers.

---

# Reading Production Chains

Always read chains from top to bottom.

Example:

```text
Grain Farm

↓

Mill

↓

Bakery

↓

Bread
```

Each building depends entirely on the previous one.

If one stage stops:

every following stage also stops.

---

# Bottlenecks

The weakest link determines total production.

Example:

```text
Iron Mine

↓

Steel Works

↓

Weapons Factory
```

If iron production falls behind:

- steel production slows

↓

- weapon production slows

↓

- warehouse empties

↓

- consumers experience shortages

Adding more weapon factories does **not** solve an iron shortage.

---

# Balanced Production

A balanced chain produces approximately the same amount at every stage.

Example:

```text
100 Iron

↓

100 Steel

↓

100 Weapons
```

Imbalances create either shortages or unnecessary stockpiles.

---

# Overproduction

Overproduction causes:

- unnecessary maintenance
- wasted workforce
- increased warehouse traffic

Large surpluses are useful only when preparing for:

- monuments
- expansion
- military campaigns
- exports

Otherwise, build according to demand.

---

# Parallel Chains

Many factories consume multiple goods simultaneously.

Example:

```text
Coal

+

Iron

↓

Steel
```

or

```text
Gold

+

Glass

↓

Pocket Watches
```

A shortage in either input halts production.

---

# Shared Inputs

Some resources feed many chains simultaneously.

Example:

```text
Steel

├────────► Weapons

├────────► Steam Motors

└────────► Heavy Industry
```

These shared resources often become critical bottlenecks during expansion.

---

# Regional Chains

Some finished goods require products from multiple regions.

Example:

```text
Old World

↓

Factory

+

Coffee

(New World)

↓

Engineers
```

As additional regions unlock, interregional logistics become essential because many advanced goods depend on imported raw materials. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# Electricity

Electricity dramatically improves industrial production.

Benefits include:

- increased productivity
- fewer factories required
- lower overall maintenance per unit produced
- higher industrial density

The guide introduces electricity as a defining milestone for engineers and advanced manufacturing. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

# Specialists

Production buildings can often be improved through specialist items.

Possible bonuses include:

- higher productivity
- reduced workforce
- reduced maintenance
- alternative inputs
- extra outputs
- additional by-products

The guide emphasizes the strategic value of Trade Union specialists for optimizing production chains. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

# Trade Union Planning

Factories with similar production should be grouped inside a Trade Union radius.

Typical heavy industry cluster:

```text
Iron Mine

Steel Works

Weapons Factory

Steam Motor Factory

Trade Union
```

This allows a single set of specialists to benefit multiple buildings.

---

# Agricultural Planning

Agriculture benefits from centralized layouts.

Advantages include:

- efficient field placement
- shorter roads
- easier warehouse access
- simpler expansion

---

# Industrial Planning

Heavy industry should prioritize:

- electricity coverage
- warehouse proximity
- Trade Union coverage
- rail access (where applicable)

Centralizing industry simplifies future optimization.

---

# Diagnosing Production Problems

When a factory stops working, check:

1. Missing input goods.
2. Workforce shortage.
3. Warehouse congestion.
4. Storage limits reached.
5. Lack of electricity (if required).
6. Transportation delays.

Never assume the problem is the factory itself.

---

# Common Mistakes

⚠️ Building downstream factories before upstream production.

⚠️ Ignoring warehouse congestion.

⚠️ Duplicating expensive factories unnecessarily.

⚠️ Producing luxury goods before demand exists.

⚠️ Mixing agriculture and heavy industry without planning.

⚠️ Expanding production without securing raw materials.

---

# Production Optimization Checklist

For every production chain:

- [ ] Raw materials available
- [ ] Intermediate goods balanced
- [ ] Warehouse nearby
- [ ] Trade Union coverage (if possible)
- [ ] Electricity available (where applicable)
- [ ] Workforce sufficient
- [ ] Storage not permanently full

---

# Key Takeaways

- Every production chain is only as strong as its weakest stage.
- Balance production instead of maximizing every factory.
- Organize industries by function to simplify logistics.
- Use electricity and specialists to increase efficiency before expanding.
- Plan for imported resources early, as advanced chains increasingly depend on goods from other regions.
