# 25 — Building Encyclopedia

> This chapter is a quick-reference encyclopedia of the major building categories presented throughout the source guide. It summarizes each building's purpose, when it becomes relevant, and important planning considerations.

> **Note:** This is a strategic reference based on the source guide—not a complete wiki containing every statistic or DLC variant.

---

# Residential Buildings

Residential buildings are the foundation of every settlement.

They provide:

- Population
- Workforce (except Investors)
- Tax income
- Progression

Progression:

```text
Farmer Residence

↓

Worker Residence

↓

Artisan Residence

↓

Engineer Residence

↓

Investor Residence

↓

Scholar Residence (Enbesa DLC)
```

Higher tiers generate more income but demand increasingly complex goods and services.

---

# Agricultural Buildings

Agricultural buildings produce raw materials.

Typical examples:

- Grain Farm
- Potato Farm
- Hop Farm
- Pepper Farm
- Vineyard
- Coffee Plantation
- Cotton Plantation
- Sugar Plantation
- Cocoa Plantation
- Tobacco Plantation
- Rubber Plantation

Planning tips:

- Leave room for fields.
- Avoid fragmenting farmland.
- Group similar farms for easier logistics.

---

# Animal Farms

Examples:

- Sheep Farm
- Pig Farm
- Cattle Farm

Provide inputs for:

- Clothing
- Soap
- Sausages
- Canned Food

Animal farms are usually best placed close to the factories that consume their products.

---

# Forestry

Buildings:

- Lumberjack Hut
- Sawmill
- Charcoal Kiln

These chains support:

- Construction
- Steel production

Timber remains useful throughout the entire game.

---

# Fisheries

```text
Fishery

↓

Fish
```

Essential early-game food source.

Simple production chain.

No additional processing required.

---

# Mines

Mining buildings include:

- Iron Mine
- Coal Mine
- Gold Mine
- Clay Pit
- Oil Well
- Gas Mine (Arctic)

Mine locations are fixed.

Island specialization is often determined by available mineral deposits.

---

# Industrial Buildings

Examples:

- Furnace
- Steelworks
- Glassmaker
- Brass Smeltery
- Concrete Factory
- Motor Assembly Plant

Planning priorities:

- Electricity
- Warehouse access
- Trade Union coverage

Heavy industry should generally be separated from residential districts.

---

# Consumer Goods Factories

Examples:

- Brewery
- Bakery
- Soap Factory
- Bicycle Factory
- Sewing Machine Factory
- Gramophone Factory
- Jewelry Factory

These buildings produce the majority of population goods.

The guide emphasizes clustering them around Trade Unions for maximum efficiency. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# Warehouse

Purpose:

- Store goods
- Supply factories
- Receive deliveries

Planning rules:

- Keep cart distances short.
- Expand storage before shortages occur.
- Add additional warehouses rather than relying on one central facility.

Warehouse placement is one of the most important optimization factors.

---

# Marketplace

Unlocked early.

Provides:

- Basic needs
- Residential progression

Nearly every residential district requires Marketplace coverage.

---

# Fire Station

Purpose:

- Fire protection

Best placed near:

- Dense housing
- Industrial districts

---

# Police Station

Purpose:

- Reduce unrest
- Maintain public order

Increasingly valuable as cities become denser.

---

# Hospital

Purpose:

- Disease control

Larger cities require adequate hospital coverage to minimize disruption.

---

# School

Required for Worker progression.

Should be placed close to residential districts.

---

# University

Unlocked with Artisans.

Supports higher-tier progression and advanced mechanics.

---

# Bank

Unlocked with Engineers.

Benefits:

- Increased income
- Engineer and Investor support

Banks become more valuable as high-tier residential districts expand.

---

# Trade Union

Purpose:

Optimize production buildings.

Possible bonuses:

- Productivity
- Additional goods
- Workforce reduction
- Maintenance reduction
- Alternative recipes

Factories should be deliberately arranged inside the Trade Union radius.

---

# Town Hall

Purpose:

Optimize residences.

Possible effects:

- Higher population
- Lower consumption
- Higher happiness
- Increased income

Dense residential districts maximize Town Hall value.

---

# Harbormaster's Office

Purpose:

Optimize harbor buildings.

Possible bonuses:

- Faster loading
- Better repairs
- Reduced maintenance
- Additional goods

Important trade hubs benefit greatly from Harbormaster coverage.

---

# Arctic Lodge

Exclusive to **The Passage** DLC.

Functions similarly to:

- Trade Union
- Town Hall

But only affects Arctic buildings. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Harbor Buildings

Major harbor structures:

- Trading Post
- Depot
- Pier
- Repair Crane
- Harbor Defenses

Planning priorities:

- Loading capacity
- Storage
- Future expansion

Busy ports often require multiple piers.

---

# Shipyard

Produces:

- Cargo ships
- Military ships
- Steam ships

Large empires eventually operate multiple shipyards.

---

# Oil Harbor

Purpose:

Receive oil.

Supports:

```text
Oil Harbor

↓

Railway

↓

Power Plant
```

Oil logistics become essential after Engineers unlock electricity.

---

# Power Plant

Purpose:

Provide electricity.

Coverage planning is critical.

Factories outside the electrical radius lose major productivity bonuses.

---

# Railway

Connects:

- Oil Harbor
- Power Plants

Railways should be planned before cities become too dense.

---

# Museum

Displays archaeological collections.

Provides:

- Attractiveness
- Collection bonuses

---

# Zoo

Displays animal collections.

Supports:

- Attractiveness
- Tourism

---

# Botanical Garden

Displays botanical collections.

Provides:

- Attractiveness
- Collection bonuses

Together, these three buildings form the cultural core of a late-game city.

---

# World's Fair

One of the largest monuments.

Benefits:

- Prestige
- Rewards
- Cultural importance

Requires substantial resources to complete.

---

# Research Institute

Exclusive to **Land of Lions**.

Purpose:

- Research

- Item creation
- Empire optimization

- Airships

# Building Placement Principles

For nearly every building, prioritize:

1. Warehouse access
2. Future expansion
3. Specialist coverage
4. Electricity (if applicable)
5. Efficient road connections

Good placement saves far more money than rebuilding later.

---

# Building Audit

For every new building, ask:

- [ ] Is it close to the buildings it serves?
- [ ] Is there room to expand?
- [ ] Will it benefit from specialists?
- [ ] Does it have warehouse access?
- [ ] Does it require electricity?
- [ ] Will it create future bottlenecks?

---

# Key Takeaways

- Every building should fulfill a specific strategic role.
- Residential, industrial, logistical, and cultural districts should generally remain distinct.
- Warehouses, Trade Unions, and Power Plants are among the most influential buildings for long-term efficiency.
- Harbor infrastructure becomes increasingly important as regional trade expands.
- Planning for future growth is almost always more efficient than rebuilding later.
